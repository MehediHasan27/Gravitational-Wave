GW260730 · 62 M☉ · 4.1 BILLION LIGHT YEARS

For a fifth of a second, three solar masses stopped being matter and became a wave. It crossed four billion years of vacuum, passed through the Earth, and stretched this page by one part in 1021.

01 — Propagation

What the wave does to
everything it touches

i

Strain

Space itself lengthens along one axis and shortens along the other, then swaps, at the frequency of the orbit. Nothing moves through space. Space moves.

ii

Chirp

As the orbit tightens, frequency climbs as (tc−t)−3/8. The same curve drives the pitch you hear and the spin you see. One function, both senses.

iii

Ringdown

The remnant is briefly the wrong shape. It rings like a struck bell at its quasinormal modes, sheds the asymmetry, and settles into perfect Kerr silence.

02 — Detection log

0.2 seconds, four billion years late

The two bodies had been circling each other for an age, radiating orbital energy away as gravitational waves and falling inward to pay for it. That is the whole story: a slow leak, then a fast one, then none.

In the final second the separation collapsed from a few hundred kilometres to nothing. Orbital velocity reached over half the speed of light. The accretion flow — the hot, magnetised gas you can see above — was sheared into arms, torn between two wells that were about to become one.

At peak, the merger radiated more power than every star in the observable universe combined. It emitted almost no light.

What arrived here was a strain of 10−21. Over the four kilometre arm of an interferometer that is a displacement one thousandth the width of a proton. We measured it. Twice, on two continents, seven milliseconds apart.
